## 1. Author-locked routing-centric center

**One-sentence thesis:** For CVE-aligned NVD–GHSA record pairs across four
fields, three frozen routing strategies produce different conflict-escalation,
abstention, and total manual-route allocations; independent trained-analyst
judgments test whether those deterministic differences correspond to
differentiated maintenance actions or expose an empirical decision boundary.

The stable research object is field-level maintenance routing for frozen,
CVE-aligned NVD–GHSA record pairs. Human judgments are the validation instrument
and validity gate, not the object named by the title. A field mismatch remains
an observation rather than a factual conflict, ground-truth label, or maintenance
recommendation.

The minimum sufficient story has two layers. E01/E07B support a reproducible
four-field discrepancy census and deterministic three-strategy queue comparison.
E08/E09, if valid, determine whether the observed routing differences align with
two trained analysts or expose a bounded decision/identifiability limit.

## 3. Author-locked neutral research questions

- **RQ1 -- Deterministic discrepancy landscape.** Across 8,066 CVE-aligned
  NVD–GHSA record pairs, how do deterministic field statuses distribute for
  severity, affected versions, publication date, and references?
- **RQ2 -- Deterministic routing comparison.** How do three frozen routing
  strategies—a strong field-aware simple comparator, a current type-first
  candidate, and an abstention-aware type-first candidate—allocate field
  instances across actions, and where do their conflict-escalation, abstention,
  and total manual-route outputs differ across fields and statuses?
- **RQ3 -- Analyst-bounded validation.** When two independent trained analysts
  assign maintenance actions to the same frozen formal cases, do their
  judgments differentiate the three routing strategies in a consistent
  direction, and where do reliability, agreement, coverage, abstention, or
  shared-miss boundaries emerge?

RQ1 and RQ2 are answered only with deterministic, snapshot-bounded outputs.
RQ3 requires E08/E09. None presupposes reliability, correctness, superiority,
non-inferiority, safety, workload reduction, or a positive human outcome.

## 5. Result branches

### Branch P: reviewer-consistent strategy differentiation

Use this branch only if both reviewers independently clear every frozen
reliability, paired-direction, event-floor, coverage, and manual-loss gate. The
permitted conclusion is limited to the frozen sample and these trained
analysts. Do not convert queue counts into saved labor, deployment safety, or
universal superiority.

### Branch B: decision or identifiability boundary

Use this branch if calibration terminates, reliability is insufficient,
reviewers favor different strategies, event floors are not met, manual-route
coverage fails, or systematic shared misses appear. Retain field-specific
disagreements, abstentions, uncertain outcomes, and failed gates. Do not add
cases, drop fields, alter thresholds, or revise policies after exposure.

### Common result under either branch

RQ1/RQ2 and C1/C2 remain deterministic, descriptive, and reproducible. RQ3/C3
reports either analyst-bounded differentiation or the observed boundary. The
paper does not need a positive policy result to preserve the deterministic
findings.
